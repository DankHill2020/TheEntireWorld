# Support Operations

Launch requires a monitored support address/portal, a public status location, a
named owner and backup, hours of coverage, response targets, privacy-safe identity
verification, escalation, and incident messaging. Do not ask customers to send
project assets or source files for licensing support.

Document playbooks for email verification, account recovery, organization admin
changes, agreement/acceptance history, license selection, named-seat assignment,
lost-machine deactivation, device-limit disputes, offline expiry, payment/refund,
tax invoice, project registration, Community reporting, Enterprise escalation,
accessibility, and privacy deletion/access requests. Administrative overrides
must be least-privilege, time-bound where possible, reason-coded, and audited.

Publish only support levels the team can actually deliver. Self-service support
needs maintained documentation and a response channel; Enterprise SLAs need
staffing, on-call coverage, exclusions, and service-credit language approved in
the agreement. Define severity consistently and never promise 24/7 coverage
before it exists.

Verification evidence includes working inbound/outbound tests, staffing rota,
escalation contacts, response targets, canned status templates, admin-access
review, and completion of a lost-device plus account-recovery drill.

## Staff-light launch model

The pre-revenue model is self-service first, with one named primary owner and one
emergency backup rather than a large support team. The private account portal
provides masked device history, self-service device deactivation, structured
case intake, customer case history, and owner triage. A credential-free status
page and hourly synthetic watchdog separate service outages from account-specific
problems.

Support intake is intentionally bounded. It accepts controlled categories and
owned opaque record IDs, not attachments, free-form logs, project paths, source
files, scenes, animation, or other creative content. Duplicate open requests are
collapsed to reduce queue noise. Device replacement does not require staff when
the customer can deactivate the old activation.

The primary reviews normal-priority cases once each business day. Only security,
account lockout, payment disputes, privacy requests, provider outages, and signed
Enterprise commitments require direct human handling. The backup covers urgent
events and primary unavailability. Do not advertise continuous coverage, phone
support, or contractual response times until those obligations are funded and
written into the controlling agreement.

This design reduces staffing but does not eliminate responsibility. Before the
support control can be verified, protected evidence must identify the real
primary and backup, prove that urgent notifications reach both, and record one
lost-device plus account-recovery exercise.
